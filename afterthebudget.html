01The Read

Six things to carry into daily executive briefings, ordered by consequence for banking clients rather than by date.

Act

The Bank of England is expected to raise Bank Rate to 4.00% on Thursday 5 November

Markets price a 25bp rise at about 94% and 16 of 17 research houses expect it, but the MPC has not decided. In September it held at 3.75% by 6–3, with three members already voting for 4% (the Bank's page; the three names are from secondary sources). September CPI came in at 3.5%, core 2.8% and services 3.7%, above the 3.4% poll. The OBR assumes a rising Bank Rate. Parliament is in recess from 5 to 9 November, so there is no Commons reaction until Tuesday 10 November. Chief Economist Huw Pill and Fergal Shortall brief on Friday 6 November at 12:15.

Brief as: priced, not decided. Pre-build a hike and a hold. The story is the vote split and the guidance for February, not the direction. See Rates and data.

Act

The bank tax is a temporary 6% surcharge, and the argument about extending it has started

The Budget raises the bank corporation tax surcharge from 3% to 6% for 15 months, from 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2028, a 31% headline rate on in-scope profits. The OBR scores it at about £1.5bn net over three years. It is smaller and shorter than the 8% that had been feared. Lloyds, NatWest and Barclays closed 1.6% to 2.0% higher on the day (reported), after intraday gains of 3% to 4%. The Treasury says the end date will be written into Finance Bill 2026-27; UK Finance, the CBI and TheCityUK want that honoured, while the TUC wants it extended or raised. The Commons votes the Budget resolutions on Tuesday 3 November. No Finance Bill date is published.

Brief as: "the Treasury says it is temporary". Do not write that it will end in March 2028. See Budget landed.

Watch

A possible leak of the surcharge decision is now a Treasury Committee question

Bloomberg reported at 10:45 on Budget day that the surcharge would be below 8% and temporary, ahead of the statement at about 12:30. Committee chair Meg Hillier wrote that day to the Treasury's permanent secretary and the FCA's chief executive asking whether this was an improper release of inside information. Replies are requested "as soon as possible" with no date. Healey is expected to be questioned in November; no date is published. The Committee takes Budget evidence on Tuesday 3 November and from the OBR on Wednesday 4 November at 14:15.

Brief as: "the chair has asked whether". Do not say banks were tipped off or that a leak occurred.

Watch

NatWest reports tomorrow at 07:00; Lloyds has already reported and its shares fell

Lloyds (29 Oct): nine-month statutory profit before tax £6.6bn (+42%), 2026 guidance maintained, CFO William Chalmers called the doubling "unhelpful" to growth and hopes it is temporary. Shares fell about 4% to 6% in early trading; sources split on whether the cause was slower 2027 net interest income growth or tax. No new motor finance charge. For NatWest the consensus is about £2.2bn operating profit (reported) and its full-year RoTE guidance is above 19%. One outlet estimates about 85% of Lloyds and NatWest profits are in surcharge scope against 33% at Barclays and 20% at HSBC (reported, single source).

Brief as: first NatWest comment on the 6%, NII guidance, deposit competition. Do not attribute Lloyds' share fall to the Budget. See Results.

Watch

Motor finance now has a date: the Upper Tribunal hearing is 16–26 February 2027

The FCA's statement of 13 October fixes a nine-day hearing on the four challenges to the redress scheme and drops the December option. A judgment date is not published; the FCA says only "in the following months". The scheme stays partly suspended, and the FCA has told lenders to plan for the scheme being quashed, which would put complaints back on normal handling rules. Q3 results added no new charges: Lloyds says its provision remains its best estimate (about £1.95bn is a June trade-press figure, not restated in the Q3 text), Barclays holds £430m, Santander UK reports roughly £616m to £633m (sources conflict).

Brief as: hearing date confirmed, outcome and judgment date unknown. Never quote a payment date.

Note

Customers and households: the Halifax shutdown, the Google outage and a consumer squeeze

The Halifax app and online banking stop on Sunday 1 November, with the last day to switch to Lloyds on Saturday 31 October (reported; Lloyds' own page not read). Expect copycat scams and service load on Monday 2 November. The 20 October Google Cloud outage hit Revolut, Lloyds/Halifax and HSBC; the Treasury Committee asked for an initial reply by Wednesday 4 November (reported). GfK consumer confidence fell 11 points to -24, the biggest fall since April 2020. House-purchase approvals fell to 53,443, the lowest since December 2023. The average two-year fixed mortgage rate is about 5.97%.

Brief as: reported, with the date of each report. Check the bank's own statement before quoting any outage or closure story.

Bank tax: what is known and what is not

Known (with status)

  • CONFIRMED The surcharge rises from 3% to 6% for 15 months, 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2028, then reverts to 3% from 1 April 2028. With 25% corporation tax the headline rate on in-scope profits is 31%, up from 28%. Budget document
  • CONFIRMED Yield in Table 6.1: £305m in 2026-27, £950m in 2027-28, £220m in 2028-29, about £1.475bn in total. The OBR and Commons Library say "around £1.5bn". Commons Library
  • CONFIRMED The measure is in Finance Bill 2026-27 (measure 1.37, "Increase in Rate of Banking Surcharge for Temporary Period"). Accounting periods that straddle 1 January 2027 are split. Policy paper
  • REPORTED The Treasury says the rise "will not be extended". One research strand read this in the Budget document; another found it only quoted by the Guardian. Treat as REPORTED until the Finance Bill text is out.
  • REPORTED Allowance of £100m and about 65 banking groups in scope. One reading of the policy paper lists both; another could not confirm the allowance on a publisher page.
  • REPORTED The OBR figure is net of about £190m of assumed profit-shifting. Brit Brief
  • REPORTED No reserves-interest tax or separate windfall levy found in secondary coverage. The full Table 6.1 was not read line by line, so a minor financial-services measure cannot be ruled out.
  • REPORTED About 85% of Lloyds and NatWest profits are in scope, against 33% at Barclays and 20% at HSBC (one outlet). The Market Reporter

Not known

  • When Finance Bill 2026-27 is introduced and gets its second reading. The Commons votes the resolutions on Tuesday 3 November; the rule is second reading within 30 days (arithmetic: about 3 December, not a published date).
  • Whether building societies are in scope, and the exact allowance.
  • Whether the Treasury and the FCA reply to the Treasury Committee on the 10:45 Bloomberg report, and when Healey appears. Replies are asked for "as soon as possible"; no date.
  • How much of the cost banks pass into mortgage and loan pricing. Lloyds' CFO called the rise "unhelpful" to growth; the Telegraph reports a warning that it will push up borrowing costs.
  • Whether the extension argument gains weight. The TUC wants it extended or raised; UK Finance and the CBI want the end date honoured.
  • How NatWest, the most exposed lender, reads it tomorrow.
Attribution rule. "Temporary" is the Treasury's word, so write "the Treasury says". On the 10:45 report, write "the Committee chair has asked whether", never "banks were tipped off". Exposure shares, the TUC's views and trade-body estimates belong to their authors.

Library fixes suggested by this scan. ONS monthly GDP did not fall on a Wednesday in either month: Thursday 12 November carries the Q3 first estimate, and Friday 11 December the October estimate. 11Briefing desk

For analysts building daily RI executive briefings for banking clients: reference numbers, wording rules, what to pre-build and what to check each morning.

Reference numbers as at 29 October

Wording rules for the window

  • Priced is not decided. Say "markets price", "16 of 17 research houses expect"; never "the Bank will raise".
  • Temporary is the Treasury's word. Write "the Treasury says it will not be extended". Do not write that it ends in March 2028 as fact.
  • Mention is not event. The Bloomberg 10:45 report is a question from the Committee chair, not a finding.
  • Intraday is not close. Budget-day bank moves were 3% to 4% intraday and 1.6% to 2.0% at the close. For Lloyds on 29 October, no close was found.
  • Separate voices. Government, the Bank, trade bodies (UK Finance, CBI, TheCityUK), unions (TUC) and think-tanks (IFS, Resolution Foundation) each get their own attribution.
  • Motor finance: "hearing 16 to 26 February 2027; judgment date not published; scheme partly suspended". Quote provisions as each firm states them. Never quote a payment date. Santander UK's figure is £616m in press and £633m in the RNS text.
  • Use the new names. Lloyds House Price Index (formerly Halifax); Standard Life plc (formerly Phoenix). The Halifax brand is being retired from 1 November (reported).
  • Clocks changed on Sunday 25 October. All times in this edition are GMT unless stated.
  • Debt measures differ. The Library's PSND is 95.5% to 97.3% of GDP; ONS PSNFL is 82.7%. Name the measure.

Pre-build checklist

  • By Fri 30 Oct 06:30: NatWest results template with the 6% surcharge, NII, deposit and RoTE lines, set against Lloyds on 29 October.
  • By Fri 30 Oct: a Halifax shutdown and scam-warning note for Monday 2 November.
  • By Mon 2 Nov: Tuesday's resolutions vote and the 09:45 Treasury Committee session; vote time not published.
  • By Tue 3 Nov: MPC hike, hold and split-vote versions with the Monetary Policy Report lines; the OBR at the Committee on Wednesday 14:15.
  • By Mon 9 Nov: data-run templates for 12 Nov GDP, 17 Nov labour market, 18 Nov CPI, 20 Nov public finances and retail sales, with the comparators in the table above.
  • By Mon 23 Nov: Financial Stability Report brief for Thursday 26 November.
  • By Fri 27 Nov: Black Friday and Cyber Monday scam note.
  • By Mon 7 Dec: 11 Dec GDP and the 17 Dec MPC lead-in.
  • Agree cover for the weeks of 2 November and 16 November, the heaviest by register load. Peak days are Friday 30 October, Thursday 12 November and Wednesday 18 November.

Morning checks (07:00, Monday to Friday)

  • Commons business statement: first one after recess is Thursday 12 November; it would carry any Finance Bill or Financial Services and Markets Bill second reading.
  • Votes and Proceedings and bills.parliament.uk for the Finance Bill after the 3 November vote.
  • Treasury Committee page for added sessions and published replies on the Budget report and the Google outage.
  • FCA site and Upper Tribunal listings for a motor finance statement; CMA case page for the strategic review decision.
  • Lloyds, NatWest and Nationwide newsrooms for house-price and barometer release dates.
  • The bank's own statement before quoting an outage, closure or job-cut story.
